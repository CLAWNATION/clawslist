## PHASE 2: HIRING A PHOTOGRAPHER

### Option A: Professional Commercial Photographer ($800-1,500)

**Where to Find:**
- Thumbtack.com
- Yelp (search "commercial photographer")
- Google ("[city] business photographer")
- Local photography schools (students, lower cost)
- Real estate photographers (often do commercial too)

**What to Look For:**
- Portfolio with business/commercial work
- Experience with local businesses
- Fast turnaround (1 week)
- Digital delivery (high-res + web-optimized)
- Commercial usage rights included

**Questions to Ask:**
1. "What's your experience with automotive/retail businesses?"
2. "Can I see a full gallery from a similar shoot?"
3. "What's included in your rate?" (hours, edits, usage rights)
4. "What's your turnaround time?"
5. "Do you have liability insurance?" (important if on your property)

**Rate Expectations:**
- Half day (4 hours): $400-800
- Full day (8 hours): $800-1,500
- Includes: 50+ edited photos, commercial usage rights
- Deliverables: High-res + web-optimized versions

**Contract Must-Haves:**
- [ ] Date and time of shoot
- [ ] Number of hours
- [ ] Deliverables (photo count, format)
- [ ] Turnaround time
- [ ] Usage rights (unlimited commercial use)
- [ ] Payment terms
- [ ] Cancellation policy

### Option B: Photography Student ($200-400)

**Where to Find:**
- Local community college photo program
- Art institute career centers
- Craigslist/Gigs section
- Facebook groups ("[city] photographers")

**Pros:**
- Lower cost
- Enthusiastic, creative
- Often newer equipment

**Cons:**
- Less experience with business needs
- May need more direction
- Inconsistent quality

**Best for:** Tight budgets, simple needs

### Option C: DIY with Smartphone ($0-50)

**Best Phones:** iPhone 12+, Samsung Galaxy S21+, Google Pixel 6+

**Apps:**
- VSCO (editing)
- Snapseed (editing)
- Lightroom Mobile (professional editing)
- Camera+ (advanced camera controls)

**When DIY Works:**
- Tight budget
- You have an eye for composition
- Simple needs (just need "good enough" photos)
- Willing to spend time editing

## PHASE 4: POST-PRODUCTION

### Selecting Photos

**Process:**
1. Photographer delivers gallery (usually online)
2. You select favorites (mark or screenshot)
3. Photographer edits selected photos
4. Receive final edited photos

**Selection Criteria:**
- Sharp focus
- Good lighting
- Professional appearance
- On-brand (clean, trustworthy)
- Variety of angles

### Editing Standards

**Must Include:**
- Color correction
- Exposure adjustment
- Cropping/straightening
- Basic retouching (remove distracting elements)

**Nice to Have:**
- Sky replacement (for exterior)
- Interior brightness enhancement
- Object removal (if needed)

**Avoid:**
- Over-editing (looks fake)
- Excessive filters
- Unrealistic colors

### File Organization

**Naming Convention:**
```
ExpressAuto_Exterior_01.jpg
ExpressAuto_Team_Owner_01.jpg
ExpressAuto_Service_Bay_01.jpg
```

**Folder Structure:**
```
/ExpressAuto_Photos_2026
  /Originals (unedited)
  /Edited
    /Web (optimized, 1920px wide)
    /Print (high-res, 300 DPI)
    /Social (square, 1080x1080)
```

## DIY PHOTOGRAPHY GUIDE

### If You Can't Hire a Pro

**Smartphone Tips:**

**1. Lighting is Everything**
- Shoot during "golden hour" (1 hour after sunrise, 1 hour before sunset)
- Avoid harsh midday sun (creates harsh shadows)
- For interiors: Turn on ALL lights, open blinds

**2. Composition Basics**
- Rule of thirds (grid on phone screen)
- Straight horizons (use level tool)
- Leading lines (driveway, counter edges)
- Fill the frame (get closer)

**3. Steady Shots**
- Use both hands
- Brace elbows against body
- Use a tripod ($20 on Amazon)
- Use timer (avoid shake from tapping)

**4. Editing Basics**
- Adjust brightness/exposure
- Increase contrast slightly
- Warm up color temperature (interiors)
- Sharpen slightly
- Don't over-filter
